# How Many People Search for What Innovive Sells

**Prepared by:** Volado Labs, October 15, 2026
**For:** Victoria Zimmerman and Robin Gaffney
**Question from the October 1 call:** Search Console tells us how often Innovive showed up in Google. How many people search for these products at all, and can we see where they are?

---

## Summary

- **The market is small, and Innovive already shows up for a large part of it.** Across the 23 product terms below that have a usable estimate, people in the U.S. ran about 8,800 Google searches from July through September 2026 (roughly 2,900 a month). Innovive appeared in the results for about 3,500 of those, or about 4 in 10.
- **Showing up is not the problem. Ranking high enough to get the click is.** Those 3,500 appearances turned into about 34 clicks. On most of these terms Innovive sits around position 7 to 25, which is the bottom of page one or later. People rarely click that far down.
- **"Disposable mouse cages" is the best example of the opportunity.** About 20 people a month search it in the U.S. Innovive appeared about 41 times in the last 90 days (U.S. only) against roughly 50 searches, so it was there for most of them, and got 7 clicks. That is a strong click rate for a term this small, and it shows the buyers are real.
- **The biggest gaps are terms where Innovive barely appears at all:** "rodent caging" (about 390 U.S. searches a month, Innovive essentially absent), "rodent rack" (about 170 a month, absent), and "laboratory mouse cages" (about 170 a month, appearing only for about 4% of searches, far down the page).
- **Geography:** we can show search volume by country with reasonable confidence. U.S. state or city numbers technically exist but are useless for terms this small (every state comes back as "10", the lowest number Google reports). Search Console only reports by country. The details are below.

---

## How to read the numbers

- **Monthly searches** come from Google's own advertising planner (Google Ads), pulled through DataForSEO. It is the same source every SEO tool relies on. It is an estimate, and for small terms Google rounds heavily: you will see 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 70, 90, 110, 140, 170 and so on, never 23 or 61. "10" means "somewhere from a handful to about 15".
- **Google combines near-identical phrases.** "Mouse cage" and "mouse cages" get one shared number. So in the table we count Innovive's appearances for all the close spellings of a term together (for example "IVC cage", "IVC cages", "individually ventilated cage" and "individually ventilated cages").
- **Innovive appearances and clicks** come from Google Search Console, U.S. searchers only, July 14 to October 11, 2026 (90 days). An "appearance" (Google calls it an impression) means Innovive's site was on a results page the person actually loaded. It does not mean they scrolled to it or noticed it.
- **Share** = Innovive's U.S. appearances divided by U.S. searches over the same three months (July, August, September). Read it as "roughly what fraction of the people searching this term could have seen Innovive." Where the share is over 100%, Google's estimate of searches is simply too low for that term, which happens a lot with small numbers. Treat those as "Innovive shows up nearly every time."
- **Some terms mix lab buyers with pet owners.** "Mouse cage", "rat cage", the water bottle terms and "rat rack" are searched heavily by people with pet mice, rats and snakes. We kept them because Innovive gets real appearances there, but the true lab-buyer share of that demand is much smaller than the raw number. They are marked "mixed" below.

### What the table says

1. **Innovive is visible, but usually not high enough.** Shares of 50% to 80% look healthy, but on most of those terms Innovive is at position 7 to 10 or lower. Clicks only come when Innovive is near the top: "disposable mouse cages" (position 4 in the U.S., 7 clicks) and "IVC rack" (position 2, 1 click on very low volume).
2. **The vivarium services terms are tiny but Innovive already owns them.** Innovive appears more often than Google's estimate says people search, which tells us the real number is a little higher than 10 to 30 a month, and that Innovive is shown nearly every time. These searches have not produced a click in 90 days, so the page that appears (and its title and description in Google) is worth reviewing. This matches what came up on the October 1 call about the San Diego "vivarium services" search.
3. **Three clear gaps to target:** "rodent caging" (390 a month), "rodent rack" (170 a month), and "laboratory mouse cages" (170 a month). Together that is about 730 U.S. searches a month where Innovive is almost invisible. These are plain category words that lab buyers use, and they are the biggest growth opportunity in this list.

### What we cannot show, and why

- **U.S. states and cities: available in principle, useless in practice for these terms.** Google's planner will return numbers for a single state or metro area. We tested California and Massachusetts, two of the biggest life-science markets. Every term came back as 10 or 20 per month, including terms like "disposable mouse cages" where the whole country is only 20. California plus Massachusetts cannot each be 10 if the national figure is 20 and there are 48 other states; that is Google putting a floor on small numbers, not real data. For niche lab terms, state and city numbers carry no information, and we recommend not showing them.
- **Search Console only reports by country.** It never says which state or city a searcher was in. There is no setting that changes this.
- **The better source for U.S. geography is the website itself.** Google Analytics records the state and city of visitors who arrive from Google search (for visitors who accept analytics cookies). That shows where interested people actually are, even though it only counts people who clicked, not everyone who searched.
- **If Innovive runs Google search ads on these terms,** the ad account's location report shows actual ad appearances by state and metro. That is the most accurate state-level view of real demand Google offers, because it counts real searches instead of rounded estimates.

## Recommendation for the dashboard

1. **Add a "Search demand" section with one row per term group** (the bold groupings in the table above, not every spelling). For each group show: U.S. searches per month, Innovive appearances, Innovive clicks, and two shares: "appeared for" (appearances divided by searches) and "clicked" (clicks divided by searches). The second share is the one that will move as rankings improve.
2. **Show search counts as ranges, not exact numbers,** for example "10 to 20 a month" rather than "17". That keeps anyone from reading precision into Google's rounding. Cap the "appeared for" share at "nearly every search" when it goes over 100%.
3. **Use the same calendar months for both sides.** Part of the confusion on October 1 (66 appearances on the dashboard against 49 in Search Console) came from comparing different date ranges and different country settings. Every number in this section should say its period and whether it is U.S. only. For reference, "disposable mouse cages" had 52 appearances worldwide and 40 in the U.S. over the last 90 days.
4. **Keep pet-heavy terms out of the headline totals** ("mouse cage", "rat cage", the water bottles), or show them in a separate "mixed audience" group, so the market size is not inflated by hobbyists.
5. **Geography: show country only.** A small table with U.S., U.K., Canada, Germany, France and Japan is enough. Do not show state or city search volume. If the team wants a U.S. map, build it from Google Analytics visitors from search, labeled clearly as "visitors" rather than "searches".
6. **Refresh monthly.** Google publishes the search estimates once a month, so more frequent updates add nothing. Each refresh costs about 10 cents in data fees for the U.S. and roughly a dollar for all countries.

---

## Caveats

- Google's search estimates for terms under about 100 a month are rough. Use them for order of magnitude ("tens", "hundreds"), not for month-to-month changes.
- Search Console hides some very rare searches for privacy, so Innovive's true appearance and click counts are slightly higher than shown.
- "Appeared" includes appearances on page two or three for people who scrolled there. It is a measure of reach, not of being seen.
- Google's estimates count Google searches only, not Bing or AI assistants such as ChatGPT. Those are a small but growing share of how researchers look things up.

*Sources: Google Ads search volume via DataForSEO (pulled October 15, 2026; U.S., U.K., Canada, Germany, France, Japan, India, California, Massachusetts); Google Search Console property innovive.com, July 14 to October 11, 2026. Data cost: about $0.90.*
