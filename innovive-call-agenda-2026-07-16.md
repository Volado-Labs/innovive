### NetSuite Connection (raise this, do not commit to an approach)

Your e-commerce runs on NetSuite, so your real customer and order history already lives there. HubSpot is not worth much to your sales team until it can see that history. Connecting the two is what makes the CRM real rather than an empty database.

HubSpot publishes a native NetSuite connector. It is included on all HubSpot plans, and it syncs contacts, companies, deals against opportunities, invoices, orders, products, support cases, and activities, one-way or two-way per object. If it fits, this is close to free and takes weeks, not months.

**What we do not know yet, and cannot know from the outside.** Three answers decide whether this is a free connector or a five-figure project:
1. Whether Innovive runs NetSuite OneWorld with multiple subsidiaries (Innocycle, InnoPlus, Nextbeam). The native connector syncs to a single subsidiary only. Multiple subsidiaries forces middleware.
2. Whether the storefront is SuiteCommerce.
3. Whether Rasan can enable REST Web Services, OAuth 2.0 with token-based auth, and server-side RESTlets. This needs NetSuite Administrator rights and is not something we can do.

**On the call:** say we are scoping the NetSuite connection as part of the roadmap and we need 20 minutes with Rasan before we can price or promise it. Ask Robin to make that introduction. Do not name a cost or a timeline today.

**Internal note:** if it turns out to be the native connector, this is a large amount of client-visible value for very little effort, and it is the strongest argument that HubSpot work is worth the retainer. If it is OneWorld multi-subsidiary, the honest answer is that it becomes a real project (Celigo runs roughly $12K-$25K per vendor pricing) and should be scoped and sold separately rather than absorbed.

**Deliverable:** Henry is drafting the implementation roadmap. We'll send it after this call.

## 3. Organic Content Program (research briefs)

**The problem we're solving:** Content has stalled. The concern raised internally was that posts read as AI-written, and the practical bottleneck is that your subject matter experts have the knowledge but not the time to start from a blank page.

**New approach.** We stop writing the posts and instead do everything up to the writing:
- Keyword research and competitive ranking analysis
- Content pillar planning, so posts ladder up to a strategy instead of being one-offs
- A detailed research brief and outline per post: the angle, the structure, the questions to answer, the sources, the search terms it targets

Your SME writes from the brief. The words are genuinely theirs, which solves the "sounds like AI" objection at the root. The brief means they're filling in expertise, not inventing structure.

**Cadence:** Two briefs per week, delivered for review.

**What we need from you:** A named SME per pillar and a realistic commitment on turnaround. The briefs only create value if something gets published.
