# What Research Ready Means for Your Science and People

Research ready means every component of your vivarium caging system arrives sterile, pre-bedded, and fully assembled so your team can go from delivery to active research without washing, autoclaving, or assembling a single cage. For Innovive customers, it also means the people running the facility work in a safer, more ergonomic environment that eliminates the physical toll of traditional cage processing.

## Quick Answer

Research ready is the principle behind Innovive's disposable IVC system. On the science side, cages ship irradiated, pre-bedded, and double-bagged with your choice of bedding, water, and enrichment, so they are ready for animals the moment they arrive. On the people side, the system removes cage washing entirely, which cuts repetitive strain, heavy lifting, and the need for a dedicated dirty-side facility. The result: faster study setup, better biosecurity, and healthier staff.

## What Does Research Ready Actually Mean?

The term gets used loosely in the industry, but at Innovive it refers to a specific operational reality: nothing needs to happen between receiving your caging and housing animals. No washing. No autoclaving. No bedding. No water bottle filling. No assembly.

Every [Innocage](https://www.innovive.com/mouse-caging) ships pre-bedded with your choice of corn cob, Biofresh, or ALPHA-dri bedding. Each cage is irradiated and sealed in double bags before it leaves the facility. Pre-filled [Aquavive water bottles](https://www.innovive.com/aquavive-mouse-water) arrive in three options: acidified, chlorinated, or ultra-purified. If your protocol calls for enrichment, pre-enriched cages with Innorichment are available too.

This is not a convenience add-on. It is the entire operating model. When your caging arrives research ready, you can skip the cage wash room, the autoclave queue, the bedding station, and the water-filling line. Your team goes straight to work.

## How Does a Research Ready System Benefit Your Science?

The obvious benefit is speed. A traditional washable caging workflow means receiving dirty cages, washing them, drying them, inspecting them, adding bedding, adding water, and then placing them on racks. That process can take hours or even a full shift before a single cage is available for animals.

With a research ready system, you skip all of those steps. Cages go from the loading dock to the rack in minutes.

But there is a less obvious and arguably more important benefit: consistency. Every cage that arrives from Innovive has been processed through the same irradiation protocol at the same co-located eBeam facility. The bedding depth is consistent. The water volume is consistent. The sterility assurance level is consistent. That kind of uniformity across thousands of cages per delivery reduces a variable that most facilities accept without questioning.

Traditional cage wash operations introduce variability at every step. Water temperature fluctuations, inconsistent detergent dosing, human error in bedding application, and contamination risks during the transition from clean to dirty sides all add noise to your research environment. A peer-reviewed study published in the Journal of the American Association for Laboratory Animal Science found that disposable IVC systems using irradiated caging maintained lower ammonia levels compared to washable systems, which directly affects animal health and data quality [1].

When you are running studies where environmental control matters, and that is most studies, eliminating those variables matters.

## What Makes the Irradiation Process Different?

Innovive built a co-located eBeam irradiation center specifically for processing vivarium components. This is not a third-party service or an outsourced step. The irradiation happens on-site, which means Innovive controls the entire chain from manufacturing to sterilization to packaging.

eBeam (electron beam) irradiation works by directing a focused beam of high-energy electrons at the product. It inactivates microorganisms by breaking molecular bonds in their DNA, making it one of the most effective sterilization methods available [2]. The process is fast, does not require chemicals, and does not leave residues.

For facilities that run barrier housing or immunocompromised animal studies, this level of sterility assurance is not optional. The double-bagged, irradiated delivery method described in peer-reviewed literature on IVC biocontainment confirms that sterility is maintained from production through to cage opening in the vivarium [3].

Every shipment comes with irradiation certificates and bioload testing results, giving your quality assurance team full traceability without additional validation steps on your end.

## How Does Research Ready Improve Life for Vivarium Staff?

This is the part that does not show up on a spec sheet but changes how people feel about coming to work.

Cage washing is one of the most physically demanding tasks in any animal facility. Traditional washable cages are heavy. They require lifting, stacking, loading into industrial washers, unloading, inspecting, and redistributing, often hundreds of times per shift. The ILAR Journal has documented that the combination of force, frequency, and awkward posture in animal care tasks contributes directly to repetitive strain injuries [4]. The CDC notes that animal care workers face risks from "strains, sprains, back injuries, or other repetitive motion injuries when lifting, restraining, or treating animals" [5].

Innovive's system removes cage washing from the workflow entirely. No dirty side. No wash room. No autoclave loading. The cages are single-use and recyclable, so once they are used, they go to [Innovive's recycling service](https://www.innovive.com/sustainability), not back through a wash cycle.

The cages themselves are made from thin-wall PET plastic, which makes them significantly lighter than traditional polycarbonate or polysulfone washable cages. Third-party ergonomic analysis has confirmed that the Innovive system reduces ergonomic risk factors for vivarium technicians. The [Innorack system](https://www.innovive.com/how-it-works) uses wheeled racks, so moving full racks of cages requires less physical effort than fixed shelving or non-wheeled alternatives.

In a field where staff turnover is a known and persistent challenge, reducing the physical burden on animal care technicians is not a minor operational tweak. It is a retention strategy.

## What Does Eliminating Cage Wash Really Change?

The cage wash suite is one of the most expensive and complex areas of any vivarium. It requires specialized HVAC, high-demand plumbing, steam generation, industrial-grade washers and dryers, and dedicated floor space. It also requires trained staff to operate and maintain all of that equipment.

When you remove cage washing from your facility plan, the downstream effects are significant:

**Capital savings.** No cage wash suite means no industrial washers, no steam generators, no specialized plumbing. For new builds, this can translate to millions of dollars in avoided CapEx.

**Space recapture.** The area that would have been a dirty corridor, wash room, and clean staging area can be repurposed for additional holding rooms, procedure space, or support areas.

**Reduced mechanical complexity.** Fewer systems to maintain means fewer breakdowns, fewer service contracts, and lower long-term operating costs.

**Simpler facility design.** Eliminating the dirty-to-clean workflow means you do not need separate corridors, airlocks, or pressure differentials dedicated to cage processing.

Innovive has published a detailed [facility planning checklist](https://www.innovive.com/post/the-cage-wash-free-vivarium-a-facility-planning-checklist-to-reduce-cage-wash-infrastructure-and-ca) for institutions evaluating a cage-wash-free approach. For facilities already in operation, the [Inno+ vivarium management service](https://www.innovive.com/inno-plus) provides daily cage changing, health monitoring, and sanitization using the research ready system, including weekends and holidays.

## Can You See Through Innovive Caging?

Yes, and this is a bigger deal than it sounds.

Innovive cages are made from thin-wall, optically clear PET plastic. Unlike traditional polycarbonate cages that yellow over repeated autoclave cycles, or polysulfone cages that carry a slight amber tint, Innovive cages are transparent from the first use. Because they are single-use, they never degrade from repeated washing and heat exposure.

The practical impact: vivarium staff can observe animals without removing cages from the rack. For many study types, this means fewer cage disturbances, which is better for the animals and better for the data. Staff can do visual health checks, assess nesting behavior, and monitor food and water levels by walking the rack line rather than pulling individual cages.

This reduces handling time per cage, which adds up quickly across a large facility. It also reduces the number of times animals are disturbed during a study, which is a direct benefit to animal welfare and study integrity.
