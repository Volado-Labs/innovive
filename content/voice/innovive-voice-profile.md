# Innovive Voice Profile

Built 2026-11-03 from the 11 posts live on innovive.com and from what Innovive's reviewers said about our drafts (Robin, Victoria, Jeremy and Ann, Sept 2026). Not every live post is a model. Read the sources note at the end before borrowing from one.

## Voice summary

Innovive writes like a vivarium operator who built a better system and is explaining it to another operator. The prose is plain, concrete and calm. It assumes the reader runs or plans an animal facility, so it never explains the basics. It speaks as "we" and states what the Innovive system does as fact, without hype and without hedging.

## Core personality traits

- **Peer, not teacher.** The reader is a vivarium director, facility manager, attending veterinarian or planner. They know what an IVC, IACUC, cage wash, census, autoclave and cage change are. Never define them. Explain only what is specific to Innovive or genuinely new.
- **Grounded in the room.** Openings put the reader in the physical, operational world: the racks, the washroom, the schedule, the study start. "Steel racks filled the room." "In a vivarium, time off is never abstract." It is never an abstract formula or a clever aphorism.
- **Confident from our side.** Innovive invented consumable IVC caging in 2006 and says so. Benefits are stated plainly ("Cages arrive irradiated and ready to use"). We never sound like a neutral analyst weighing vendors, and we never coach the reader toward reusable caging.
- **Practical and specific.** Real product names, real numbers, real workflow steps. Each claim is something a facility manager could act on or check.
- **Calm.** No exclamation, no drama, no marketing adjectives. Strength comes from specifics, not intensifiers.

## Tone spectrum

| Dimension | Position |
|---|---|
| Formal ↔ Casual | Professional, conversational. Contractions are fine but sparing. |
| Reserved ↔ Bold | Confident about our system, measured about everything else |
| Simple ↔ Sophisticated | Plain words for an expert reader. No dumbing down, no jargon for effect |
| Warm ↔ Direct | Direct, with warmth when the topic is about people (staff, burnout, leave) |

## Vocabulary

**Use:** consumable IVC caging, consumable caging (preferred in body copy); disposable (H1, first 100 words and meta only, for search); Research-Ready; irradiated; double-bagged; animal-naive (never housed another animal); cage wash, washroom, tunnel washer, autoclave; census; cage change; holding room; study start; one-way flow; closed-loop recycling, InnoCycle; end-of-use or used caging; 100% PET (resin code 1), BPA-free. Product names exactly: Innocage, Innorack, Aquavive, Inno+, InnoCycle, Innorichment, InnoWheel, InnoTube.

**Avoid:**
- "Sterile", "sterilize", "pre-sterilized". Say irradiated, and for reusable workflows say autoclave.
- "High-viscosity PET"
- "Waste" for our used caging
- Definitions of basics ("IVC stands for individually ventilated cage")
- Third-person self-reference ("Innovive says", "according to Innovive")
- Self-undermining hedges: "honest", "candid", "any vendor, us included", "whoever you buy from", "be skeptical of vendor claims"
- Hype and metaphor: "stratosphere", "game-changer", "big red X", "noisy, steaming heart", "nexus"
- Coined aphorisms that need decoding ("size from the animals outward")
- The standard AI list: landscape, comprehensive, robust, leverage, seamless, crucial, pivotal, journey, navigate, foster, unlock, elevate

**Jargon level:** the reader's own operational vocabulary, used freely. Explain only an acronym outside vivarium operations, on first use.

## Rhythm and structure

- **Sentences:** mostly 12 to 20 words, plain subject-verb-object. Use an occasional short pair for weight ("Biosecurity improved. Research consistency improved.") at most once or twice per piece.
- **Paragraphs:** 2 to 4 sentences.
- **Openings:** one or two short paragraphs that name the situation the reader is in and what this piece will help them decide. No "Quick answer" voice in the first lines, no rhetorical-question pile-ups, no "Think about your vivarium for a moment".
- **Tables:** sparse. Use them only for real numbers a reader will look up (space minimums, rack capacities). Keep them to 4 columns or fewer, with short cells and units in the header. Never put sentences in table cells. A comparison of workflows reads better as prose.
- **Close:** a plain invitation to work with our team ("Send us your census and we'll size it with you"), linked to contact or Inno+.

## POV and address

- **First person:** "we" and "our", as Innovive. Name "Innovive" only where a proper noun reads naturally.
- **Reader:** "you", sparingly. Many of the best passages describe the facility, not the reader.
- **Stance:** experienced peer and system maker.

## Examples

**On-brand**
- "Planning a new holding room usually comes down to one question from facilities or finance: how many racks, and which ones? We work it out the same way on every sizing call, starting from the census."
- "Our cages arrive irradiated, double-bagged and animal-naive, so a cage change never waits on the washroom."
- "Most of our mouse customers change cages every 14 days; rat rooms typically run 7 to 10."
- "Innoracks run from a standard outlet and do not need a room exhaust connection, so a rack rolls into place and goes into operation without plumbing work."

**Off-brand**
- "You size an IVC rack from the animals outward." A coined aphorism the reader has to decode.
- "IVC stands for individually ventilated cage." Too elementary for this reader.
- "So ask any vendor, us included, whether its cages count as sealed." A neutral-analyst hedge that undermines us.
- "That last row is the honest trade-off... whether you already own a working cage wash." This coaches the reader to stay with reusable caging.
- "Vivarium construction costs are climbing into the stratosphere." Hype.

## Do and don't

**Do**
- Start from the reader's operational reality.
- State our system's strengths as plain fact, with a source where a number is involved.
- Compare on full cost per occupied cage-week, and offer to run the numbers with the reader.
- Keep tables few, narrow and numeric.

**Don't**
- Explain the field to the people who work in it.
- Weigh vendors neutrally or invite doubt about our claims.
- Make switching, recycling or compliance sound hard.
- Name-drop agencies or studies for weight.

## Sources and which live posts to model

- **Model these:** "Wash-Driven to Research-Driven" (3991) for system confidence and concrete scene-setting; "Strategic Leave Coverage" (3981) and "The Unseen Weight" (3986) for a calm, peer tone. Note that their em dashes are not allowed in our copy.
- **Do not model these:** "Contingency Planning" (3972), "Contamination Control" (3978), "Staffing Models" (3987) and "The Cage Wash-Free Vivarium" (3989). They are heavy with stock AI phrasing, hype metaphors or abstract framing.
- **Reviewer feedback, Sept 2026:**
  - Victoria: "It should come from us," not "as a bystander."
  - Jeremy: "overly critical of our product," "like we are hiding something."
  - Ann: "cumbersome," and flagged name-dropping.
  - Robin: wants content "helpful to our business," not making switching sound "cumbersome, long, drawn out."
- **Sept 10 meeting rules:**
  - first-person plural
  - confident
  - no mechanical answer-first openings
  - sparse tables
