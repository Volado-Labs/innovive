## From washable to single-use caging in an older facility

**A case study from the MRC Prion Unit at University College London**

The MRC Prion Unit at UCL was running caging systems more than 20 years old. Its cage washer, bottle washer and water purification system all needed replacing, and there was no capital budget to replace them. Deputy Facility Manager Nicholas Kaye moved the facility to the Innovive single-use IVC system instead.

The racks were rented to own over 12 months, and the cost of the single-use cages was covered by what the facility no longer spent servicing the old equipment. Time spent processing cages and bottles fell by 80%, and that role now handles ordering and stock. The washers and cage storage no longer take up space, and lighter cages mean less heavy lifting for staff.

Nick's presentation walks through the decision itself: equipment due for replacement with no capital budget, how the switch was funded, the day-to-day impact on staff and space, and his lessons for any facility facing the same choice.
