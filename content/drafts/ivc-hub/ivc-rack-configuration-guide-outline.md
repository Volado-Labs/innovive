## Outline

### H1: How to Configure an IVC Rack for Mice and Rats

**Quick answer box (40 to 60 words, for AI Overviews):** To configure an IVC rack, work from your animal census to cages, then to racks. Choose the cage size that meets space recommendations for your species and weight, set the animals per cage, size the rack's rows and columns to your cage count and room, pick positive or negative pressure for your biosecurity needs, and plan the cage-change workflow.

**At a glance table:** the eight decisions on one screen, each with a one-line "what decides it" and a jump link to its section.

### H2: Step 1. Start from your animal census

- What to count: species, strains, average adult weight, breeding versus holding, peak census over the next 2 to 3 years, not today's.
- Table: *Guide* floor-space recommendations by weight for mice and rats, plus female-with-litter values and minimum cage heights. **Verify against the *Guide*, 8th ed., Table 3.2 before publishing.**
- Callout: the *Guide* figures are minimums; IACUCs and AAALAC programs can be stricter. Check your institution's policy.

### H2: Step 2. Choose the cage size, then animals per cage

- How floor space and headroom translate into animals per cage.
- Table: Innocage bottoms (M-BTM 81 in², M-BTM-XL 146 in², R-BTM-H 141 in², R-BTM-XL 325.8 in²) against headroom and Innovive's "sized for" numbers (5 mice, more than 5 mice, 2 rats, large rats).
- Euro Type II Long and Type III equivalents, so buyers can compare with what they run today.
- Breeding note: trios and harems need more floor space, which is where M-BTM-XL comes in.

### H2: Step 3. Work out how many cages you need

- Formula: peak census ÷ animals per cage, plus a buffer for breeding, separations, quarantine and cage changes. **The buffer percentage must come from Innovive or Inno+ operating experience.**
- Worked mini-example in a box: 600 adult mice at 4 per cage = 150 cages, plus buffer = about N cages.

### H2: Step 4. Choose the rack: capacity, sides and layout

- Single- versus double-sided: room layout, aisle access, wall placement.
- Rows and columns: how they set capacity and the height of the top row (ergonomics).
- Table, reused from the hub: Innorack IVC Mouse (up to 192 positions, 9 to 12 rows × 6 to 8 columns), IVC Rat (up to 80, 6 to 8 × 3 to 5), Tall IVC Rat (up to 60, 5 or 6 × 4 or 5), Tall IVC Rat XL (up to 24, 6 × 2).
- **Configuration matrix (the asset that makes this page worth linking to):** every row-and-column option against cages per side and per rack, plus footprint. **Needs dimensions and per-configuration counts from Innovive data sheets.**
- Room fit: footprint, aisle clearance, door widths and ceiling height for moving racks; a sketch of two double-sided racks in a standard holding room.

### H2: Step 5. Ventilation: airflow, filtration and pressure

- What an IVC rack does: per-cage supply and exhaust, and why that controls ammonia, CO₂, humidity and allergens (cite peer-reviewed sources).
- Positive versus negative pressure, as a decision table:
  - Positive: protects the animals (immunocompromised, gnotobiotic, high-value lines).
  - Negative: protects people and the room (allergen control, biohazard, infectious studies).
- HEPA on supply only versus supply and exhaust. The Innorack filters both.
- On-rack blowers versus connecting to building exhaust: what each means for HVAC, noise and moving racks.
- Air changes per cage per hour, typical ranges from the literature, and Innorack's figure. **Innovive to supply ACH, noise level and power draw.**
- Airflow design: transversal airflow (Innovive's patented approach) explained in plain words, without disparaging other designs.

### H2: Step 6. Plan the cage-change and wash workflow

- The question most guides skip: who changes cages, how often, and where dirty cages go.
- Washable caging: cage-wash capacity, labor, water and energy, and the space for clean and dirty storage.
- Disposable caging: storage space for incoming cases, the change process, recycling pickup (InnoCycle closed-loop program).
- Table: washable versus disposable, by cage-wash need, labor steps, storage, contamination risk and capital cost. **Sourced claims only; reuse the How It Works and System Benefits facts.**

### H2: Step 7. Water, feed, bedding and enrichment

- Bottles versus automatic watering, and what each means for the rack.
- Pre-filled bottles (Aquavive: acidified, chlorinated or ultra-purified).
- Pre-bedded cages (corn cob, ALPHA-dri, BioFresh) and enrichment that fits the cage height.

### H2: Step 8. Specialized studies

- Metabolic studies: the disposable metabolic cage docks in the Innorack IVC Mouse. Link to the metabolic cage page.
- Gnotobiotic programs: MVX3 lid, positive pressure, irradiated cages. Link to Gnotobiotics.
- Tall rats and large rats: when 7" of headroom is not enough (Tall IVC Rat, Tall IVC Rat XL, 9").

### H2: Step 9. Maintenance, cleaning and monitoring

- Wipe-down cleaning and the autoclavable option. Filter replacement intervals, blower service and controller alarms. **Intervals from Innovive.**
- Daily checks: the controller display, cage seating and filter status.

### H2: Worked examples

Three short scenarios, each from census to final configuration, set out as a table:
