# How to Configure an IVC Rack for Mice and Rats

You size an IVC rack from the animals outward. IVC stands for individually ventilated cage: a cage with its own filtered air supply and exhaust, held in a rack that moves the air. Here is the method, with real numbers and worked examples on our Innorack IVC racks.

> **Quick answer**
> To configure an IVC rack, work from your animal census to cages, then to racks. Choose a cage that meets space recommendations for your species and weight, set animals per cage, size rows and columns to your cage count and room, pick positive or negative pressure, and plan how cages get changed.

## Step 1. Start from your animal census

Size for the peak census you expect over the next two to three years, not today's. Count by species and adult weight, and keep breeding separate from holding.

It also shows whether you need a mouse IVC cage system, a rat system or both. A mixed census usually means mixed racks, since only the M-BTM-XL mouse cage crosses over to the IVC Rat 3.5.

Most U.S. programs work from the minimums in the *Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals* (8th edition) [1]:

| Animal | Weight | Floor area per animal | Minimum cage height |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mice in groups | Under 10 g | 6 in² (38.7 cm²) | 5 in (12.7 cm) |
| | Up to 15 g | 8 in² (51.6 cm²) | 5 in (12.7 cm) |
| | Up to 25 g | 12 in² (77.4 cm²) | 5 in (12.7 cm) |
| | Over 25 g | 15 in² or more (96.7 cm² or more) | 5 in (12.7 cm) |
| Mouse female with litter | | 51 in² (330 cm²) for the housing group | 5 in (12.7 cm) |
| Rats in groups | Under 100 g | 17 in² (109.6 cm²) | 7 in (17.8 cm) |
| | Up to 200 g | 23 in² (148.35 cm²) | 7 in (17.8 cm) |
| | Up to 300 g | 29 in² (187.05 cm²) | 7 in (17.8 cm) |
| | Up to 400 g | 40 in² (258.0 cm²) | 7 in (17.8 cm) |
| | Up to 500 g | 60 in² (387.0 cm²) | 7 in (17.8 cm) |
| | Over 500 g | 70 in² or more (451.5 cm² or more) | 7 in (17.8 cm) |
| Rat female with litter | | 124 in² (800 cm²) for the housing group | 7 in (17.8 cm) |

Height runs from cage floor to cage top. These are minimums; your IACUC or AAALAC program may set stricter ones [1][2].

## Step 2. Choose the cage size, then animals per cage

Divide a cage's floor space by the per-animal figure for adult weight and round down. That is a ceiling, not a target. The *Guide* does not count floor covered by water containers or enrichment, and your IACUC sets the final density [2].

## Step 3. How many cages do you need?

**Cages = (peak census ÷ animals per cage, rounded up) + buffer**

The buffer covers what a headcount misses: new breeding setups, animals separated for fighting or health, and positions for quarantine or change-day spares.

> **Worked mini-example**
> 600 adult mice at 4 per cage = 150 cages.
> With an illustrative 15% buffer: 150 × 1.15 = 172.5, so plan 173 positions.

## Step 5. Should the rack run positive or negative pressure?

The *Guide* says IVCs can meet the animals' ventilation needs without raising room ventilation, though the room must still handle heat, odor and gases from the cages [3].

Pressure mode decides which way air escapes: positive pushes it out of the cage, negative pulls it in.

| Mode | Protects | Typical uses |
|---|---|---|
| Positive | The animals | Immunocompromised lines, gnotobiotic programs, high-value colonies |
| Negative | People and the room | Allergen control, and biohazard or infectious studies |

Supply HEPA guards the animals and exhaust HEPA guards the room, and every Innorack filters both.

Allergen studies back the split. All six IVC systems in one study contained mouse urinary allergen more than 250-fold at negative pressure, while unsealed racks leaked at positive pressure [4]. Another found the lowest room levels with sealed IVCs in either mode, so ask any vendor, us included, whether its cages count as sealed [5]. [CONFIRM: whether Innocage IVC lids are sealed in the sense used in these studies]

The *Guide* allows well-filtered exhaust back into the room but prefers building exhaust [3]. Innoracks run on their own blowers from a standard outlet, with an optional HVAC connector to vent odor and humidity. ## Step 6. Plan the cage-change and wash workflow

In our view this is where most rack plans go wrong, and it has little to do with the rack. Who changes cages, how often, and where do the dirty ones go?

Most users of our Innocage IVC Mouse change cages every 14 days; most rat users change every 7 to 10. The *Guide* accepts longer IVC intervals when you verify the cage environment, for example by measuring ammonia and CO₂ [6].

Washable caging sends every change through the wash room, with its washers, water, energy and staff. The same allergen study found staff exposure highest during cage changing and emptying soiled cages [5].

Disposable caging ends the loop at your dock. Our cages arrive irradiated and double-bagged, never having housed another animal, and leave in their bags with the bedding inside, packed in gaylords. InnoCycle, our closed-loop recycling program, separates the bedding and recycles the plastic, targeting zero landfill ([see how the system works](https://innovive.com/how-it-works/)).

| | Washable caging | Disposable Innocage caging |
|---|---|---|
| Cage wash | Bottoms, lids and bottles | None for bottoms and lids; rat wire bars still go through a washer, autoclave or hand wipe |
| Change steps | Dump bedding, wash, sterilize, dry, restock | Open the bag, set up, change, re-stack the used cage |
| Storage | Clean and dirty cages plus the wash room | New caging ([CONFIRM: caging shelf life, correction pending from Innovive]; kept dry, under 115°F/46°C) and a recycling area, often about 10' × 10' |
| Contamination risk | Depends on wash and sterilization | Each cage arrives irradiated and unused |
| Up-front cost | Racks, cages, washers, wash room | Racks; no wash room |
| Running cost | Water, detergent, energy, labor, repairs | A cost per cage, plus a fee if you use our recycling program |

That last row is the honest trade-off: disposable caging swaps capital and wash labor for a cost on every cage you open. Which is cheaper depends on your census, labor rates and whether you already own a working cage wash, and we would rather run that math with you than assert it.

## Worked examples

All three use an illustrative 15% buffer. [CONFIRM: Innovive recommended buffer]

### 1. Mouse breeding colony, about 1,000 mice

| Step | Decision |
|---|---|
| Census | 300 breeding adults in 100 trios, plus 700 holding mice |
| Cages | Trios in M-BTM-XL; holding mice at 4 per M-BTM |
| Cage count | 100 + 15% = 115 XL; 175 + 15% = 202 M-BTM |
| Pressure | Positive, to protect the colony |

On Innorack: 2 racks of 9 rows × 6 columns, double-sided (Innorack IVC Mouse 3.5, 216 positions), plus 2 racks of 6 rows × 5 columns, double-sided (Innorack IVC Rat 3.5, 120 positions). Two 9-row racks keep the top row low. [CONFIRM: Inno+ sanity check]

### 2. Rat toxicology study, 120 rats

| Step | Decision |
|---|---|
| Census | 120 rats, expected to stay under 500 g |
| Cages | 2 per R-BTM-H |
| Cage count | 60 + 15% = 69 |
| Pressure | Negative, for allergen control |

On Innorack: 1 rack of 7 rows × 5 columns, double-sided (Innorack IVC Rat 3.5, 70 positions). [CONFIRM: Inno+ sanity check]

If males will pass 500 g, two rats need at least 140 in² against the R-BTM-H's 141, so move to the Tall IVC Rat XL or one rat per cage.

## FAQ

### What is an IVC rack?

A rack that gives each individually ventilated cage filtered supply air and its own exhaust. Innoracks HEPA-filter both.

### How many cages fit on an IVC rack?

Up to 192 on the Innorack IVC Mouse 3.5, 80 on the IVC Rat 3.5, 60 on the Tall IVC Rat and 24 on the Tall IVC Rat XL.

### Should my IVC rack run positive or negative pressure?

Positive protects the animals and negative protects people and the room. Every Innorack runs both.

### What is the difference between IVC and static caging?

An IVC gets continuous air from the rack. A static cage relies on the room and may need more frequent sanitation [6].

### Can I use my existing cages on a new IVC rack?

The Innorack is designed for Innocage caging, so plan rack and cages as one system. [CONFIRM: wording on third-party cages and racks]

### How much does an IVC rack cost?

### How do I choose an IVC cage system?

Start with the animals: size cages to your census (Steps 1 to 3), then racks, pressure and workflow (Steps 4 to 6). Mixed facilities plan mouse and rat systems side by side.

### What size rat rack system do I need?

Count cages at 2 rats per R-BTM-H, up to 500 g each. The IVC Rat 3.5 holds 80 cages at 7" of headroom; for 9", the Tall IVC Rat holds 60 and the Tall IVC Rat XL holds 24 extra-large cages.
