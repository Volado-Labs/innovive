# How to Configure an IVC Rack for Mice and Rats

Planning a new holding room, an expansion or a new program usually comes down to one question from facilities or finance: how many IVC racks, and which ones? The answer has to fit both your census and the room you have.

We work it out the same way on every sizing call. We start from the census, then settle the cages, the racks, the pressure mode and the cage-change workflow, in that order. This guide follows those steps, with the numbers we use and worked examples on our Innorack IVC racks and consumable Innocage caging.

> **Quick answer**
> Start with your peak census and a cage that gives each animal the floor space and headroom it needs. Divide the census by animals per cage and add a buffer. That cage count sets the racks, rows, columns and sides. Then choose positive or negative pressure and plan how cages get changed.

## Step 1. Start from your animal census

Plan for the peak census you expect over the next two to three years, not today's. Count by species and adult weight, and keep breeding cages separate from holding cages.

The census also tells you whether you need a mouse IVC cage system, a rat system or both. A mixed census usually means mixed racks, because only the M-BTM-XL mouse cage crosses over to the IVC Rat 3.5.

Most U.S. programs start from the space minimums in the *Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals* (8th edition) [1]:

| Animal and weight | Floor area per animal (in²) | Minimum height (in) |
|---|---|---|
| Mice, under 10 g | 6 | 5 |
| Mice, up to 15 g | 8 | 5 |
| Mice, up to 25 g | 12 | 5 |
| Mice, over 25 g | 15 or more | 5 |
| Mouse female with litter | 51 per group | 5 |
| Rats, under 100 g | 17 | 7 |
| Rats, up to 200 g | 23 | 7 |
| Rats, up to 300 g | 29 | 7 |
| Rats, up to 400 g | 40 | 7 |
| Rats, up to 500 g | 60 | 7 |
| Rats, over 500 g | 70 or more | 7 |
| Rat female with litter | 124 per group | 7 |

Metric, in order: mice 38.7, 51.6, 77.4, 96.7 or more and 330 cm²; rats 109.6, 148.35, 187.05, 258.0, 387.0, 451.5 or more and 800 cm². Heights run cage floor to top: 12.7 cm for mice, 17.8 cm for rats.

These are minimums, and your IACUC or AAALAC program may set stricter ones [1][2].

## Step 2. Choose the cage size, then animals per cage

Divide a cage's floor space by the per-animal figure at adult weight and round down. That gives a ceiling rather than a target, because the *Guide* does not count floor covered by water containers or enrichment, and your IACUC sets the final density [2].

| Cage | Floor space (in²) | Headroom (in) | Sized for |
|---|---|---|---|
| M-BTM | 81 | 5 | 5 mice |
| M-BTM-XL | 146 | 5.8 | More than 5 mice |
| R-BTM-H | 141 | 7 | 2 rats |
| R-BTM-XL | 325.8 | 9 | Large rats |

Metric: 522.6, 942, 909.7 and 2101.9 cm² of floor space; 12.7, 14.8, 18 and 22.9 cm of headroom.

The M-BTM matches Euro Type II Long and the R-BTM-H matches Type III, while the M-BTM-XL is larger than both.

By *Guide* arithmetic, the M-BTM holds up to 5 mice over 25 g and the M-BTM-XL up to 9. The R-BTM-H holds 2 rats up to 500 g, and the R-BTM-XL 4 rats over 500 g. One female with a litter fits an M-BTM; other breeding setups depend on the number of adults and litters [1]. For trios and harems, our [M-BTM-XL mouse cage](https://innovive.com/mouse-caging-m-btm-xl/) gives extra-large group housing on the standard rat rack.

## Step 3. How many cages do you need?

**Cages = (peak census ÷ animals per cage, rounded up) + buffer**

The buffer covers what a headcount misses: new breeding setups, animals separated for fighting or health, and positions for quarantine or change-day spares.

> **Worked mini-example**
> 600 adult mice at 4 per cage = 150 cages.
> With an illustrative 15% buffer: 150 × 1.15 = 172.5, so plan 173 positions.
> The 15% only illustrates the method. [CONFIRM: Innovive recommended buffer]

## Step 5. Should the rack run positive or negative pressure?

The *Guide* notes that IVCs can meet the animals' ventilation needs without raising room ventilation, though the room still handles heat, odor and gases from the cages [3].

Positive pressure pushes air out of the cage and protects the animals, which suits immunocompromised lines, gnotobiotic programs and high-value colonies. Negative pressure pulls air in to protect people and the room, the usual setting for allergen control and biohazard or infectious studies.

All six IVC systems in one allergen study contained mouse urinary allergen more than 250-fold at negative pressure, while unsealed racks leaked at positive pressure [4]. Another found the lowest room levels with sealed IVCs in either mode [5]. Our system's cage lids create an airtight seal. [CONFIRM: whether Innocage IVC lids are sealed in the sense used in these studies]

The *Guide* allows well-filtered exhaust back into the room but prefers building exhaust [3]. Innoracks run on their own blowers from a standard outlet, with a power draw similar to a standard light bulb, and an optional HVAC connector vents odor and humidity. ## Step 6. Plan the cage-change and wash workflow

In our experience, this is where most rack plans go wrong, and it has little to do with the rack. The plan has to say who changes cages, how often, and where used caging goes.

Most of our mouse customers change Innocage IVC cages every 14 days, and rat rooms typically run 7 to 10. The *Guide* accepts longer IVC intervals when you verify the cage environment, for example ammonia and CO₂ [6].

With washable caging, every change sends bottoms, lids and bottles to be dumped, washed, autoclaved, dried and restocked, with clean and dirty storage beside the washroom. That loop carries water, detergent, energy, labor and repairs, and one study found staff allergen exposure highest during cage changing and emptying soiled cages [5].

With consumable caging, the loop ends at your dock. Our cages arrive irradiated, double-bagged and animal-naive. A change means opening the bag, setting up and re-stacking the used cage. Used caging leaves in its bags, bedding inside, packed in gaylords. InnoCycle, our closed-loop recycling program, separates the bedding and recycles the plastic, targeting zero landfill ([see how the system works](https://innovive.com/how-it-works/)).

Storage covers new caging, kept dry and under 115°F/46°C, and a recycling area of about 10' × 10'. [CONFIRM: caging shelf life, correction pending from Innovive] Rat wire bars are reusable and go through a washer, an autoclave or a hand wipe.

The fair comparison is full cost per occupied cage-week. Consumable caging is priced per cage, plus our recycling program fee if you use it, with no washroom to build or staff. We will run those numbers with you against your census.

## Worked examples

All three use an illustrative 15% buffer. [CONFIRM: Innovive recommended buffer]

### 1. Mouse breeding colony, about 1,000 mice

The colony has 300 breeding adults in 100 trios and 700 holding mice. Trios take 115 M-BTM-XL cages with the buffer, and holding mice at four per M-BTM take 202. All four racks run positive to protect the colony.

On Innorack: 2 racks of 9 rows × 6 columns, double-sided (Innorack IVC Mouse 3.5, 216 positions), plus 2 racks of 6 rows × 5 columns, double-sided (Innorack IVC Rat 3.5, 120 positions). Two 9-row racks keep the top row low. [CONFIRM: Inno+ sanity check]

### 2. Rat toxicology study, 120 rats

Housing 120 rats under 500 g at two per R-BTM-H takes 60 cages, or 69 with the buffer. The rack runs negative for allergen control.

On Innorack: 1 rack of 7 rows × 5 columns, double-sided (Innorack IVC Rat 3.5, 70 positions). [CONFIRM: Inno+ sanity check]

If males will pass 500 g, two rats need at least 140 in² against the R-BTM-H's 141, so we would move to the Tall IVC Rat XL or one rat per cage.

### 3. Small gnotobiotic program

Housing 150 germ-free mice at four per M-BTM, with the MVX3 lid, takes 38 cages, or 44 with the buffer. The rack runs positive for bioexclusion.

On Innorack: 1 rack of 9 rows × 6 columns, single-sided (Innorack IVC Mouse 3.5, 54 positions), with room to grow. With 6 hours of MVX3 planning time, we would put it on backup power. [CONFIRM: Inno+ sanity check]

## FAQ

### What is an IVC rack?

It is the frame, blowers and controller that feed filtered air to each cage and pull its exhaust. Every Innorack HEPA-filters both supply and exhaust and runs on its own blowers.

### How many cages fit on an IVC rack?

Up to 192 on the Innorack IVC Mouse 3.5, 80 on the IVC Rat 3.5, 60 on the Tall IVC Rat and 24 on the Tall IVC Rat XL.

### Should my IVC rack run positive or negative pressure?

Run positive to protect the animals and negative to protect people and the room. Every Innorack runs both.

### What is the difference between IVC and static caging?

An IVC cage gets continuous air from the rack, while a static cage relies on the room and may need more frequent sanitation [6].

### Can I use my existing cages on a new IVC rack?

The Innorack is designed for Innocage caging, so we plan the rack and the cages as one system. [CONFIRM: wording on third-party cages and racks]

### How much does an IVC rack cost?

We provide sized quotes rather than list prices, built on your census and caging model.

### How often should IVC rack filters be changed?

It depends on the rack model and the room. [CONFIRM: Innorack filter replacement interval] Our technical support team (support@innovive.com) confirms the schedule for your racks.

### How do I choose an IVC cage system?

Start with the animals and size cages to your census (Steps 1 to 3), then choose racks, pressure and workflow (Steps 4 to 6). Mixed mouse and rat facilities plan both side by side.

### What size rat rack system do I need?

Count cages at 2 rats per R-BTM-H, up to 500 g each. The IVC Rat 3.5 holds 80 cages at 7" of headroom, and for 9" the Tall IVC Rat holds 60 and the Tall IVC Rat XL holds 24 extra-large cages.
