## How Does the Cage Perform on the Rack?

A cage is only half of an IVC (individually ventilated cage) system. Our Innorack racks deliver dual HEPA-filtered ventilation with individual airflow to each cage. They come in single- and double-sided configurations, and a digital controller runs them in positive or negative pressure mode [4]. Each rack plugs into a standard outlet, draws about as much power as a standard light bulb, and restarts with its prior settings after an outage. No room exhaust connection is required [5].

Our cages also have published, peer-reviewed data behind them. A 2022 study in the Journal of the American Association for Laboratory Animal Science (JAALAS) used Innovive cages, lids, and Aquavive water. Ammonia stayed below the NIOSH 8-hour recommended exposure limit for humans (25 ppm) through day 21 for group-housed mice and day 30 for singly housed mice. Microbial bioburden on cage accessories and water testing was acceptable up to 28 days after cage change [6]. Its stated purpose was to set husbandry and sanitization performance standards, which is exactly what your attending veterinarian wants to see.

For rats, researchers at the US Army Medical Research Institute of Infectious Diseases ran our rat cages on an Innovive rack at 60 air changes per hour. Corncob bedding gave nearly twice the service life of alpha-cellulose, and corncob cages showed no detectable ammonia until day 14 [7].

## What Will Your Team Need to Learn?

Less than most managers expect. Anyone who has worked with IVCs already knows the handling. What changes is room flow: where the outer bag comes off, and opening the inner bag under the laminar flow hood where cage changes happen [1].

Our [video training library](https://innovive.com/video-training/) covers Innocage handling, docking and undocking, bottom-only changes, and Innorack controller airflow and calibration [8]. Bottom-only changes deserve a look: your team swaps the bottom and keeps the lid, feeder, and water bottle in service [8]. Our Inno+ team also offers staff training [9].

Your staff also shed some of the hardest work in the building. With no cage washing, the repetitive-strain tasks of the washroom go away, and the cages and pre-filled water bottles are light [3].

## What Happens to Used Caging?

This is where a product becomes a program. Our answer is InnoCycle, a closed-loop recycling service. Your staff re-stack used cages with the soiled bedding left in place, pack them into the bags they came in and then cardboard gaylords, and we pick them up on your schedule [5]. Most customers set aside roughly a 10 ft by 10 ft area to stage cages for pickup [5].

At the recycling facility, a separator pulls out the bedding. The cages are flaked and granulated, and clean flake goes to a vendor network for other products. Bedding goes to municipal composting where it is available [4]. The closed-loop service collects and processes 100% of returned plastic, and none of that plastic goes to landfill [5].

The material makes this work. The cage bottom, lid, water bottle, and feeders are 100% PET (resin code 1), BPA-free, and free of glue [5]. Innocage bottoms already contain up to 30% post-consumer recycled plastic, thermoformed at high heat and then fully irradiated [5].

Getting started takes one email to info@innovive.com. We set up a standard recycling contract, estimate your material volume, and build a pickup schedule around your current census [5]. Regulated or biohazard material stays in your institution's own stream, as it does today.

## How Should You Run a Pilot?

Keep it simple. Choose one representative room and place new cohorts into the system rather than moving animals mid-study. Agree on success criteria with your attending veterinarian before the first cage arrives. Track ammonia at your planned change interval, staff time per change, order accuracy, and animal health.

Once the pilot room runs well, facilities usually phase consumables in room by room, and each new room follows a routine your staff already knows. We help design the pilot, train the team, and set up supply and recycling for that first room from day one.

## FAQ

### What should I ask an IVC caging vendor first?

Ask what documentation ships with each cage. You want to know whether caging is irradiated, whether it is animal-naive, and whether you can retrieve a Certificate of Irradiation by lot number yourself. We make every COI available online by lot [2].

### Do single-use IVC cages control ammonia as well as washable cages?

Published research says they can do better. In a 2008 JAALAS study with each cage type on a positive-pressure rack, ammonia in reusable cages began rising on day 4 and reached about 50 ppm by day 5, while single-use cages stayed at or below 3.2 ppm [11]. A 2022 study using our cages kept ammonia below 25 ppm through day 21 for group-housed mice [6].

### How long does it take to switch to consumable caging?

Most facilities start with a single pilot room and new cohorts, then add rooms as each one settles in. With Inno+ services on the standardized Innovive system, a program can be operational in as little as three weeks [9].

### How long can I store Research-Ready caging?

Caging supplies keep for 1 year in their original packaging, in dry storage below 115 F (46 C) [5]. Pre-filled ultra-pure water is good for 1 year, and acidified or chlorinated water for 18 months from the fill date [5].

### Can consumable IVC cages be recycled?

Yes. Our cages are 100% PET, accepted wherever open-neck PET containers are recycled, and our InnoCycle closed-loop service collects and processes 100% of returned plastic [5]. You stack used caging with the bedding in place, and we pick it up on your schedule.
