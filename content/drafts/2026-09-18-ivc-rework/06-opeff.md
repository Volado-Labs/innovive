# Operational Efficiency Gains from Disposable IVC Caging: Labor, Workflow, and Infrastructure

Ask any vivarium manager where the hours go and cage wash comes up fast. Dirty cages go to the wash room, and clean ones come back, all day. That loop sets your headcount and your square footage. In a busy month, it also sets how far census can climb.

Disposable IVC caging takes the loop out of the day. We designed Innocage and our Research-Ready program so your technicians open a bag and set up a cage instead of rebuilding one. The time they get back goes to animal care and to the science your facility exists to support.

## Quick Answer

Consumable IVC caging removes the wash, dry, reassemble, and autoclave work from husbandry. Technician hours move back to animal care, and capacity stops depending on washer throughput. Cage supply becomes a planning task, which matters most at peak study loads, when a wash room would normally set the ceiling.

## Where Does Technician Time Go Once the Wash Loop Is Gone?

Cage washing is a labor block with a fixed shape: transport, dump, wash, dry, reassemble, autoclave, store. Someone breaks cages down, loads and unloads the washer, puts lids, feeders, and bottles back together, and carries clean stock back to storage. Every day, whether the rooms are full or half empty.

With consumable caging, that block is gone. Research-Ready cages arrive irradiated, double-bagged, and animal-naive, meaning they have never housed another rodent [1]. You can order them empty, pre-bedded with corn cob, ALPHA-dri, or BioFresh, pre-enriched with Innorichment, and paired with pre-filled Aquavive water [1]. The outer bag comes off wherever it suits your flow, and the inner bag is usually opened under the laminar flow hood where changes happen [1].

Your people feel the difference physically, too. Without cage washing, the repetitive-strain tasks that come with it disappear. Our cages and pre-filled bottles are lightweight, and a third-party analysis found the Innovive system reduces ergonomic risk [2]. The full list of [system benefits of our caging](https://innovive.com/system-benefits/) goes further, into biosecurity and scalability.

## What Changes During a Cage Change?

The change itself will look familiar to any technician. Animals move into a fresh cage under the hood. What's different is everything on either side of that moment. Nobody assembles clean stock beforehand, and nobody hauls the dirty cage to the wash room afterward. The used cage is restacked with its bedding intact and set aside for recycling.

Bottom-only changes make it lighter still. Your team can swap just the bottom and keep the lid, feeder, and water bottle. Our [video training library](https://innovive.com/video-training/) walks through it step by step [3].

Interval is where we get the most questions. Most Innocage IVC Mouse users change every 14 days, and most Innocage IVC Rat users change every 7 to 10 days [4]. Published data from our cages backs that up. A 2022 peer-reviewed study run on Innovive cages, lids, and Aquavive water found ammonia stayed below the 25 ppm recommended 8-hour exposure limit for people through day 21 for group-housed mice and day 30 for singly housed mice. Microbial bioburden on cage accessories and in the water stayed acceptable up to 28 days after the change [5].

Bedding choice stretches service life further. In a 2016 study using our rat cages on our rack, corncob bedding gave nearly twice the service life of alpha-cellulose and showed no detectable ammonia until day 14 [6].

Your change interval is still a welfare and performance decision that your program reviews and owns. Consumables change the work around each change, and that work gets a lot smaller.

## How Does Peak Load Work When Supply Replaces Wash Throughput?

A reusable program hits a wash ceiling in a busy month. You can only process so many cages per shift, and that number quietly caps how fast census can grow. Then a washer goes down midweek, or second shift is two people short, and clean stock runs dry right as changes come due.

Consumable caging moves the limit to supply planning, which is far easier to scale. Adding capacity becomes an order, not a construction project. A washer repair or a renovation no longer stops census. Caging supplies keep for a year in their original packaging in dry storage under 115 F (46 C), so carrying a buffer ahead of a big study is simple [4]. We plan the peak forecast with you so stock is on the shelf before the animals arrive.

We think this is the strongest operational reason to switch. Your growth stops depending on a machine.

The Center for Infectious Disease Research saw it play out, reporting increased vivarium cage capacity, reduced full-time equivalent (FTE) staffing needs, and lower utility and caging costs with our system [7]. You'll find more examples on our [case studies page](https://innovive.com/case-studies/).

## Which Infrastructure and Training Demands Shrink?

Cage wash is expensive because of what feeds it. The NIH Design Requirements Manual sizes wash areas to census, caging type, change frequency, and staffing, then adds dedicated heated water or steam, drainage, clean and dirty separation, and allowance for equipment heat and humidity [8]. Each of those is a construction line item and a standing maintenance cost.

Consumable caging needs no fixed washroom infrastructure, which frees floor space and lowers upfront investment [2]. Innorack IVC racks plug into a standard outlet, draw about as much power as a light bulb, restart with their prior settings after an outage, and don't have to connect to room exhaust [4].

End-of-use handling takes very little of your team's time. With InnoCycle, our closed-loop recycling partner, technicians restack used cages with the bedding still in them, and pickups run on your schedule [9]. Most customers set aside roughly a 10 ft by 10 ft area for staging [4]. At the recycling facility, bedding goes to municipal composting where available, and 100% of the returned plastic is processed, none of it landfilled [9]. Regulated or biohazardous material stays in your institution's own stream.

Training changes focus. Hours that went to washer operation and cage assembly move to setup technique, updated SOPs, and inventory control, and the video library gives every new hire the same starting point [3].

## FAQ

### How much labor does consumable IVC caging save?

It saves the hours your wash loop takes today. Transport, washing, drying, reassembly, autoclaving, and restocking all come off the cage workload. We measure your baseline with you so the savings are modeled on your census.

### Can we change cages less often with consumable caging?

Your program sets the interval based on welfare and cage performance. Most Innocage Mouse users change every 14 days, and some already go longer with fewer than five mice per cage [4]. A peer-reviewed study in our cages found ammonia stayed under the human exposure limit through day 21 for group-housed mice [5], so your team has a published margin to work with.

### Do we still need a cage washer for rodent caging?

Not for Innocage. Cages arrive irradiated and ready to use, and used cages go to recycling. Cage bottoms are built for single use and aren't meant to be washed [4].

### What happens to cage changes if the washer or power goes down?

A washer failure can't stop a consumable cage change, because no washer sits in the path. Innorack restarts with its prior settings after a power outage, and we publish power-loss planning guidelines by lid, such as 12 to 24 hours for the MVX1 [4].

### How fast can we get started?

With the standardized Innovive system, our Inno+ team can be operational in as little as three weeks [10]. Many facilities bring consumable caging in room by room, which keeps training focused.
