# How Disposable IVC Caging Protects Animal Health Status, Biosecurity, and Research Integrity

One health-status break can cost a facility months. Rooms go into quarantine, studies stall, and a line your investigators spent years building may need rederivation. Most of the risk lives in the everyday handoffs, and in a reusable caging program one of the biggest handoffs is the cage itself: every bottom goes through washing, autoclaving, storage, and staging before it comes back to the rack.

Disposable individually ventilated cage (IVC) caging takes that circuit out. We designed Innocage so that every cage entering your rooms is irradiated, double-bagged, and animal-naive, and no used cage ever comes back through a shared wash loop. Your colonies and your data get a cleaner, documented starting point at every cage change.

## Quick Answer

Consumable IVC caging protects health status by removing the shared wash-and-return loop, so each cage change starts with an irradiated, double-bagged cage that has never housed another animal. Peer-reviewed work using our cages and water found ammonia and bioburden within acceptable limits for weeks after a cage change [1]. Paired with HEPA-filtered racks and lot-level documentation, it gives your biosecurity program fewer uncontrolled inputs to manage.

## How Does Consumable Caging Reduce Contamination Opportunities?

Rogers and colleagues set out to establish husbandry and sanitization performance standards for single-use IVC mouse cages, and the cages, lids, and Aquavive water in their study were ours. Ammonia stayed below 25 ppm, the 8-hour recommended exposure limit for humans set by the National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health, through day 21 for group-housed mice and day 30 for singly housed mice. Microbial bioburden on cage accessories and in the water was acceptable up to 28 days after cage change [1].

Reusable caging looks very different. In a 2008 study that housed mice in disposable and reusable ventilated cages, each on a positive-pressure rack, ammonia in the reusable cages started climbing on day 4 and passed 150 ppm at the rear sampling port by day 9. The single-use cages never went above 3.2 ppm [2]. IVCs as a category already carry the lowest risk of cage-to-cage infectious spread among common caging types [3].

The rest comes down to what our cages have never been through. Our [Research-Ready caging](https://innovive.com/research-ready/) arrives irradiated, double-bagged, and guaranteed never to have housed another rodent [4]. Your team removes the outer bag wherever it fits your flow and opens the inner bag under the laminar flow hood where cage changes already happen [5]. On the rack, Innorack delivers dual high-efficiency particulate air (HEPA) filtered air to each cage individually, with positive and negative pressure modes [5]. Every lot comes with a Certificate of Irradiation (COI) you can pull by lot number [6].

Irradiated, double-bagged, and animal-naive is a specification we document lot by lot. It gives your colony health program the strongest starting point we know of, because every cage arrives with no history.

## What Does It Mean for Immunocompromised and High-Value Colonies?

Nude, SCID, NSG, and humanized models are exactly where a health-status break costs the most. The animals are expensive, often slow to replace, and highly susceptible to opportunistic agents that a healthy colony would shrug off.

That is where removing reprocessing pays off most. No cage in those rooms has been anywhere before. It never sat in a staging area next to returning dirty stock, and its cleanliness has nothing to do with how the washer ran that morning. Research-Ready options arrive pre-bedded, pre-enriched with Innorichment, or with pre-filled Aquavive water, so fewer components are handled in-house before they reach the hood [4].

Our caging fits right alongside the broader agent panels your immunocompromised barriers already run.

## What About Outbreaks and Contamination Events?

When an agent turns up, your attending veterinarian and biosafety team lead the response. Our job is to make sure clean caging is the one thing they never have to worry about.

Sealed inbound stock is ready without waiting on wash turnaround. Caging supplies keep for one year in their original packaging with dry storage below 115 F (46 C), so you can keep a reserve for exactly this kind of week [9]. Used cages from affected rooms leave the facility instead of rejoining a shared clean-equipment stream, and they can be autoclaved for decontamination using a protocol your Innovive representative can walk you through [9].

In routine operation, InnoCycle picks up used caging with the bedding still in it and recycles it through our closed-loop program [5]. Regulated or biohazard material follows your institution's own disposal stream, which your Environmental Health and Safety (EHS) team already runs.

## How Does It Affect Health Monitoring and Research Integrity?

Documented inputs make for cleaner data. Every Research-Ready cage traces to a lot, and many facilities file its COI with their study records for audits [6]. When a reviewer asks what went into a cage, you have a document to hand them.

Cage-level filtration also opens up modern monitoring methods. A 2024 systematic review of 42 studies found that environmental health monitoring has a strong evidence base and appeared superior to soiled-bedding sentinels for pathogen detection [10]. In single-use IVC systems with cage-level filtration, filter-media sampling picked up several agents about as well as cage-filter polymerase chain reaction (PCR) testing, and did better than direct sentinel sampling [11].

Facilities moving toward environmental monitoring validate it on their own racks against their own target agents, and we help with that work. Done well, it can mean fewer sentinel animals on your racks.

## FAQ

### What does Research-Ready caging guarantee?

Every Research-Ready cage is irradiated, double-bagged, and animal-naive, meaning it has never housed another rodent [4]. Each lot has a Certificate of Irradiation you can look up by lot number [6]. Your colony health program starts from that documented baseline at every change.

### Can I house immunocompromised mice in single-use IVC caging?

Yes. Consumable caging on HEPA-filtered Innorack systems suits immunocompromised colonies well, because every cage is animal-naive and nothing returns through a shared wash loop [4][5]. It works alongside the broader agent panels your barrier program already runs.

### How long can mice stay in an Innocage between changes?

Most users change Innocage IVC Mouse cages every 14 days, and some extend that with fewer than five mice per cage [9]. Published work using our cages found ammonia under 25 ppm through day 21 for group-housed mice [1]. Set your interval with your veterinary team based on census and bedding.

### Does consumable caging help during an outbreak?

It takes clean caging off your list of worries. Sealed stock can sit on the shelf for up to a year, and used cages leave the facility rather than returning to a shared wash stream [9]. Your veterinary and biosafety teams stay in charge of the response.

### Can we use environmental health monitoring with single-use IVCs?

Yes, and the evidence supports it. Filter-media sampling in single-use IVC systems with cage-level filtration outperformed direct sentinel sampling for several agents [11]. We help you validate the method on your racks and target agents before you retire sentinels.
