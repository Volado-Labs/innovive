# Sustainability and End-of-Use Management for Disposable IVC Caging Programs

Sooner or later, someone from your sustainability office asks the question every disposable IVC caging program gets: where does all that plastic go? We have a clear answer. Used Innocage caging comes back to us through InnoCycle, our closed-loop recycling program, and the returned plastic becomes feedstock for other products. None of it goes to landfill.

The more interesting part of the story is what leaves your building when you stop washing cages. A cage wash runs on steam and electricity, and it occupies floor space that has to be built, ventilated, and conditioned every day of the year. Take the wash out of the program and that load leaves with it.

## Quick Answer

Innocage bottoms, lids, water bottles, and feeders are 100% PET (resin code 1) and BPA-free, and cage bottoms contain up to 30% post-consumer recycled plastic. After use, your team re-stacks cages with the bedding still inside, and we pick them up on your schedule through InnoCycle, which processes all returned plastic with none sent to landfill. And because there's no cage wash, you also skip the steam, electricity, and washroom space that washing would otherwise demand.

## What Happens to Our Cages After Use?

We built InnoCycle so that end-of-use takes less effort than a cage change. Your staff re-stacks used cages with the soiled bedding intact. Nobody dumps or scrapes anything, and nothing goes near a rack washer. We pick up the stacks from your facility on a schedule that fits your census [1].

At the recycling facility, a separator pulls the bedding out of the cages. The plastic is then flaked and granulated, and the clean flake goes to a network of vendors who turn it into other products. The bedding goes to municipal composting where that's available [1][2].

The closed-loop service collects and processes 100% of the plastic you return, and none of that plastic is landfilled [2]. You can see the full sequence on our [how the Innovive system works](https://innovive.com/how-it-works/) page.

One note on scope. InnoCycle handles standard category material. Caging from studies involving infectious agents or other regulated hazards follows your institution's biosafety-approved disposal route, the same way it would with any other caging [2].

## What Are Innocage Components Made Of?

The cage bottom, lid, water bottle, and feeders are all 100% PET plastic, resin code 1, with no glue or other contaminants, and they're BPA-free [1][2].

That simple material profile is what makes recycling easy. Our components are accepted anywhere open-neck PET containers are recycled [2].

Innocage bottoms are also made with up to 30% post-consumer recycled plastic [2]. We melt the recycled material and thermoform it at high heat, and then every bottom is fully irradiated before it ships. You may notice a slight hue variation from the recycled content. It has no effect on clarity or quality [2].

That irradiation step matters for your animals, too. [Research-Ready caging](https://innovive.com/research-ready/) arrives double-bagged, irradiated, and animal-naive, meaning it has never housed another rodent [3]. So you get recycled content and a clean, animal-naive cage at every change.

Prefer to work with your own hauler? We can help you set up a local recycling program. We'll talk to your hauler directly and send samples and MSDS (material safety data sheet) certificates so they know exactly what they're accepting [2].

## Where Does a Vivarium's Environmental Footprint Really Sit?

People often picture detergent when they think about the environmental cost of washing cages. Detergent is a small piece of it. The heavy hitters are energy and space: steam generation, electricity for the washers, and the HVAC load needed to pull heat and humidity out of the wash area.

The NIH Office of Research Facilities Design Requirements Manual gives a clear picture of what a cage wash asks of a building. It calls for dedicated steam service, drainage, and planning for the heat and humidity the equipment throws off, along with physical separation of clean and dirty sides [4]. Every one of those items is infrastructure you build once and then power, condition, and maintain for the life of the facility. Sizing all of it depends on census, caging type, change frequency, schedule, staffing, and redundancy [4].

A consumable program takes that whole category off the table. With no fixed washroom to build, you free floor space and reduce upfront investment [5]. No washer draws power, and no steam line has to feed one.

The racks are light on energy, too. Innorack runs on a standard outlet with a power draw similar to a standard light bulb, and it doesn't need to connect to a room exhaust system [2]. Our [Innorack and recycling FAQ](https://innovive.com/faq/) covers the details. In our view, losing the washroom entirely, and running low-draw racks in its place, is the strongest sustainability argument for consumable caging, and it's the one most often left out of the conversation.

## How Easy Is It to Run the Program Day to Day?

Easy. Most customers set aside roughly a 10 ft by 10 ft area to stage used cages for pickup [2]. Staff typically pack bottoms and lids back into the bags they arrived in, then load those into cardboard gaylords following our packing instructions [2]. It fits into the same routine as the cage change itself.

Cage-change frequency keeps the volume predictable. Most users change Innocage IVC Mouse cages every 14 days and Innocage IVC Rat cages every 7 to 10 days [2], so your pickup schedule tracks closely with your census.

Enrolling takes one email. Write to info@innovive.com and we'll send a standard recycling contract, an estimate of your material volume, and a pickup schedule built around your current census [2]. From there, the program runs in the background.

## FAQ

### Can used IVC cages be recycled?

Yes. Innocage bottoms, lids, water bottles, and feeders are 100% PET (resin code 1) with no glue or contaminants, so they're accepted wherever open-neck PET containers are recycled [2]. Through InnoCycle, we collect and process 100% of the plastic you return [2].

### Do I have to empty the bedding before cages go back for recycling?

No. You re-stack used cages with the bedding intact, and a separator at the recycling facility removes it [1]. The bedding then goes to municipal composting where available.

### Do Innocage cages contain recycled plastic?

Yes. Innocage bottoms are made with up to 30% post-consumer recycled plastic, and each bottom is fully irradiated after thermoforming [2]. Any slight hue variation from the recycled content doesn't affect clarity or quality.

### How much space does a closed-loop recycling program need?

Most customers dedicate about a 10 ft by 10 ft area to stage cages for pickup [2]. Cages go into their original bags and then into cardboard gaylords, and we pick them up on your schedule.

### How do we sign up for InnoCycle?

Email info@innovive.com. We'll send a standard recycling contract, an estimated material volume, and a pickup schedule based on your current census [2]. If you prefer a local hauler, we can help set that up as well.
