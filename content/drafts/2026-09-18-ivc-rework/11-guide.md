# Disposable IVC Caging: The Complete Guide for Preclinical Vivarium Decision-Makers

Most vivariums run a loop nobody really chose to manage. Dirty cages go to the washroom, get washed, get staged, and come back. That loop eats floor space, utilities, and technician hours, and when census grows, the washroom sets your ceiling.

Disposable IVC caging takes the loop out. We built the Innovive system so every cage change starts with an irradiated cage that has never housed another animal, and ends with the used cage leaving your building for recycling.

## Quick Answer

Consumable IVC caging houses rodents in single-use cages that arrive irradiated, double-bagged, and ready to dock on a HEPA-filtered ventilated rack. After use, the cages go out for recycling instead of back to a cage wash. Containment still comes from the rack and your procedures. What goes away is the washroom and the hours your team spends feeding it.

## What Is Consumable IVC Caging, and How Is It Different From Washable?

Both kinds of individually ventilated cage (IVC) dock to a rack that feeds filtered air to each cage. The difference shows up at cage change. A washable cage goes back through your washroom. Ours doesn't come back at all.

Innocage arrives [Research-Ready](https://innovive.com/research-ready/): irradiated, double-bagged, and guaranteed animal-naive, meaning it has never housed another rodent [1]. You can order it empty, pre-bedded, pre-enriched with Innorichment, and matched with Aquavive pre-filled water [1]. Your team opens the inner bag under the same laminar flow hood where changes already happen [2].

Innorack delivers dual HEPA-filtered ventilation with individual airflow to each cage, run from a digital controller [2]. After the change, used caging is re-stacked with the bedding still inside and picked up on your schedule [2]. See [how the Innovive system works](https://innovive.com/how-it-works/) for the full cycle.

## What Containment Levels Can It Support?

In an IVC program, containment comes from the rack and the way your team works. Whether a cage gets washed afterward has nothing to do with it. Innorack offers positive and negative pressure modes, with HEPA-filtered airflow to every cage [2].

That fits how biosafety guidance describes animal biosafety level 2 (ABSL-2): when the risk assessment calls for it, animals go in primary containment such as solid-wall, solid-bottom cages with microisolator lids or equivalent, and ventilated caging is part of that discussion [3]. For higher-containment work, used Innocage can be autoclaved for decontamination, and your Innovive representative will give you the protocol [4]. Your risk assessment decides the rest.

The other question we hear early is power. Innorack plugs into a standard outlet, draws about as much as a light bulb, and restarts automatically with its prior settings after an outage [4]. We also publish a power-loss planning guideline for each lid: MVX6 lids are rated for 48+ hours, for example, and MVX1 lids for 12 to 24 hours [4].

## What Does It Do for Animal Health and Research Data?

Start every change with a clean cage and the microenvironment starts clean too. The published work backs that up.

In a 2008 JAALAS study that housed mice in disposable and reusable ventilated cages, each on a positive-pressure rack, ammonia in the reusable cages started climbing on day 4 and passed 150 ppm at the rear sampling port by day 9. The disposable cages stayed at or below 3.2 ppm [5].

A 2022 JAALAS study using Innocage, our lids, and Aquavive water found ammonia stayed below the 25 ppm NIOSH 8-hour human exposure limit through day 21 for group-housed mice and day 30 for singly housed mice. Bioburden on accessories and water tested acceptable up to 28 days after cage change [6]. That is data you can cite in your SOPs.

IVCs as a category also carry the lowest risk of cage-to-cage infectious spread among common caging types [7]. Our view is simple. A cage that has never held another animal is the easiest biosecurity control a facility can add.

## How Does the Cost Structure Compare?

Most of the savings come from what you never build.

| | Wash-based IVC | Innovive consumable IVC |
|---|---|---|
| Capital | Racks, durable cage inventory, cage washer, washroom build-out | Racks |
| Space | Permanent washroom | About 10 ft x 10 ft to stage used cages for pickup [4] |
| Recurring | Wash labor, water, energy, repairs | Caging ordered to census |
| Scaling | Limited by wash capacity | Order more cages |

NIH design requirements for a cage wash call for dedicated heated water or steam, drainage, equipment heat and humidity loads, and clean/dirty separation [8]. With consumable caging none of that has to exist, so you get the floor space back and spend less up front [9]. Innorack doesn't even need a room exhaust connection [4].

Your team feels it too. Cage washing is where a lot of repetitive-strain work lives, and it disappears [9].

The totals can be large. AVEO Oncology saved over $2 million by switching from washable caging, according to its customer case study [10].

AAALAC International accredits animal care and use programs, not products, and takes a performance-based approach [11]. The Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals favors performance standards too [12]. Innocage Mouse gives more than 81 sq in (530 cm2) of floor space and 5 in of headroom, which exceeds what the Guide recommends for five mice [4].

Paperwork is easy. You can look up the Certificate of Irradiation (COI) for any lot by its lot number, and many facilities keep it on file for study records and audits [13].

Through InnoCycle, our closed-loop recycling partner, used caging is picked up with the bedding still in it. The bedding is separated out, and the plastic is flaked and granulated for new products. ## FAQ

### What biosafety level can disposable IVC caging support?

Your risk assessment sets the level, and the rack does the containing. At ABSL-2, biosafety guidance describes solid-wall, solid-bottom cages with microisolator lids or equivalent as primary containment when the assessment calls for it [3]. For higher-containment work, used cages can be autoclaved per protocol [4].

### Does a disposable cage contain less than a reusable one?

No. While a cage is docked, containment comes from the rack's HEPA filtration, individual cage airflow, and pressure mode, and Innorack provides all three [2].

### What happens to disposable IVC cages in a power outage?

Innorack restarts on its own with its prior settings once power comes back [4]. Our planning guideline lists a time for each lid, up to 48+ hours for MVX6, so you can plan room by room [4].

### Can disposable IVC cages be recycled?

Yes. The cages are 100% PET (resin code 1) and are accepted wherever open-neck PET containers are recycled [4]. InnoCycle, our closed-loop program, picks up used caging on your schedule and processes 100% of returned plastic [2].

### How fast can we start using consumable IVC caging?

Research-Ready caging can go into use as soon as it arrives, or be stored until you need it [1]. If you want our team running the vivarium, Inno+ can have you operational in as little as three weeks [15].
